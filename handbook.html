About the Project

Making legal rights accessible.

One of Soch's major contributions in this area has been our involvement in creating a Handbook for Sex Workers, which provides accessible legal information and resources to a marginalised community. The Handbook was developed collaboratively, with the goal of putting practical, understandable legal knowledge directly in the hands of the people it concerns.

Round Table Consultation

October 19, 2024

Soch played an instrumental role in organising a Round Table Consultation bringing together legal experts, policymakers, academicians, activists, advocates and sex workers to address the legal and social challenges faced by the community in India. This collaborative effort marked a meaningful step toward securing dignity, equality and protection for all individuals, regardless of profession or background.

Know Your Rights

Rights during police interaction.

Right to File an FIR

No police officer can refuse to file an FIR for crimes involving violence or harassment. It can be filed anywhere, with or without jurisdiction, and a free copy must be provided.

Right to Know Grounds of Arrest

If arrested, you have the constitutional right to be informed of the grounds for arrest and where you will be held.

Right to Silence

The constitutional right against self-incrimination includes the right to remain silent. Get a lawyer before answering questions.

Right to a Magistrate Within 24 Hours

If arrested, you must be presented before a magistrate within 24 hours — you cannot be detained longer without the magistrate's permission.

Right to Notify a Relative or Friend

You must be allowed to inform a relative or friend of your arrest and the place of detention.

Right to Medical Examination

Every arrested person can demand a medical examination, and has the right to one every 48 hours in custody.

Right to Consult a Lawyer

A recognised constitutional right, including the right to meet an advocate during interrogation. Free legal aid is available through NALSA and State Legal Services Authorities.

Rights of Sex Workers

From Budhadev Karmaskar v. Union of India.

Following a Supreme Court panel report, several rights of sex workers were recognised and directed to State governments for enforcement.

Right to a Dignified Life

Sex workers are entitled to dignity under Article 21 — police must register complaints, treat them with dignity, and not subject them to abuse or coercion.

Right to Medical Assistance

Survivors of sexual assault have the right to immediate medical assistance. Using health and safety measures like condoms is not evidence of any offence.

Right to Privacy

Media must not publish or share photos or details of sex workers taken during raids.

Right to Not Be Held Against Will

Adult women cannot be detained in ITPA rescue homes against their will if they are voluntary workers.

Right to Identity Documents

Sex workers are entitled to Aadhaar and identity documents, even without standard residence proof, via State Legal Services Authority verification.

Right to Be Aware

NALSA and State Legal Services Authorities are mandated to educate sex workers about their rights and how to access the justice system.

Key Terms

Glossary.

FIR (First Information Report)

A written complaint registered by police when someone reports a crime for the first time. You can go to the police station and file one to make them take action; a zero FIR can be filed even outside the local jurisdiction.

Arrest

Taking someone into custody legally by police or another official, usually with an arrest warrant unless it's an emergency.

Custody & Detention

Being kept at a police station or elsewhere by officials. Police cannot detain someone more than 24 hours without presenting them before a magistrate.

Bail & Non-bailable Offence

Bail is release from custody while awaiting a hearing. For non-bailable offences, this isn't automatic — a judge's permission is needed.

Arrest Warrant / Search Warrant

A legal paper from a court giving police permission to arrest someone or search a place. Police should have one before entering a home, barring emergencies.

Jurisdiction

The area a police station or court has power over. A station outside the relevant area can still register a "zero FIR" and forward it.

Accused

A person said to have committed a crime — legally presumed innocent until proven guilty at trial.

Interrogation & Self-Incrimination

Police questioning. You have a constitutional right to stay silent and not be forced to testify against yourself — get a lawyer before answering anything.

Memo of Arrest

A written record police must make when arresting someone — time, place and reason — signed by the arrested person and a witness.

Medical Examination (in custody)

A check-up to record injuries. Every arrested person can request one, and is entitled to one every 48 hours in custody.

Custodial Violence

Abuse of someone while in police custody. This is illegal regardless of the alleged offence.

Public Nuisance / Public Obscenity

Broad, often loosely-applied charges — for disturbance or lewd conduct in public — that are sometimes misused against sex workers.

Notified Area / Soliciting

A "notified area" is one the government has declared prostitution-free — sex work there can be punished. "Soliciting" means asking for paid sex, usually in public, and is often broadly interpreted.

Brothel

Legally, any place with more than one sex worker is considered a brothel — maintaining one (not sex work itself) is the offence.

Raid and Rescue / Protective Home

Raid and rescue is police or NGO action removing sex workers from their workplace, often against their will. Protective/rehabilitation homes are where they may be sent afterward — courts have held that consenting adults should not be forced to stay.

Decriminalisation

Removing laws that make sex work itself a crime — a key long-term demand of the sex workers' movement, distinct from legalisation or regulation.

Consent

Saying "yes" freely, without pressure or coercion — central to distinguishing voluntary sex work from trafficking under Indian law.

Exploitation & Redressal

Exploitation is being used by others for profit without real choice. Redressal is getting justice or help afterward — filing an FIR is one form of redressal.

Sensitisation

Training people — like police officers — to treat sex workers and other vulnerable groups with dignity and understanding, to help prevent abuse.

Targeted Interventions (TI)

Government health programmes (originally for HIV prevention) that also help sex workers access identity documents like Aadhaar via a verified proforma certificate.

The Movement

Marked by sex workers, worldwide.

Sex workers around the world observe several days of action — including International Sex Workers' Day (June 2, marking a 1975 church occupation in Lyon, France), the Red Umbrella Marches (September 14, using the red umbrella as an international symbol of the movement), International Sex Workers' Rights Day (March 3), and the International Day to End Violence Against Sex Workers (December 17). In India, organisations including the Durbar Mahila Samanwaya Committee, SANGRAM/VAMP and the National Network of Sex Workers have organised for decades around health, safety and legal recognition.

Future Advocacy

What we're still pushing for.

Equal protection under law

Criminal law should apply equally based on age and consent — where a sex worker is a consenting adult, police should not interfere.

No arrest for voluntary work

Since only running a brothel (not voluntary sex work itself) is unlawful, sex workers should not be arrested, penalised or harassed during raids.

A voice in policy-making

Sex workers and their representatives should be included in decisions on policy, programmes and laws that affect them.

No forced separation from children

A sex worker's child should not be presumed trafficked or forcibly separated solely because the parent is in sex work.
